Documents Needed to Sell an Estate Home

What you'll actually need to gather before an estate, Power of Attorney, or Committee-of-Estate home sale can proceed in BC.

The short version: the exact documents depend on which situation applies — probate, Power of Attorney, or Committee of the Estate — but most estate sales require some combination of the items below. Gathering these early, before you're ready to list, saves real time later.

For a probate sale (executor or administrator)

  • Original will (if one exists) and the death certificate
  • Grant of Probate (or Grant of Administration if there's no will) — this is generally required before the Land Title Office will register a transfer
  • Court forms filed as part of the probate application, including the Notice of Proposed Application, the Submission for Estate Grant, the Affidavit of Applicant, and the Affidavit of Assets and Liabilities — your estate lawyer or notary prepares these, but it helps to know they exist
  • Property title details and any existing mortgage information
  • Current property tax records
  • A general inventory of the estate's assets and debts, which your lawyer will likely already be compiling

For a Power of Attorney sale

  • The original enduring Power of Attorney document, properly executed and witnessed
  • Confirmation the POA is filed with (or acceptable to) the Land Title Office — not every POA document qualifies; see our Power of Attorney & Real Estate guide
  • Identification matching the name on both the title and the POA document

For a Committee of the Estate sale

  • The court order appointing the Committee
  • Separate court approval specifically authorizing the sale of the real property — see our Committee of the Estate guide for why this is usually a distinct step

General documents every estate sale benefits from

  • Any existing mortgage statements or payout information
  • Home insurance details, especially confirmation of vacant-property coverage if applicable
  • Utility account information
  • Any known strata documents, if the property is a condo or townhome

Common Questions

Documents Needed to Sell an Estate Home — FAQ

Do I need the Grant of Probate before I can even list the home?
You can often begin preparing a home and even list it conditionally before the Grant is issued, but the actual transfer at the Land Title Office generally requires the Grant to be in hand. Timing this correctly is exactly the kind of planning Mark Jontz helps executors with.
What if the original will can't be found?
This typically means applying for a Grant of Administration instead of a Grant of Probate, which involves a somewhat different process — your estate lawyer handles this distinction.
Is a Power of Attorney from another province valid in BC?
Not automatically — BC has specific requirements for what the Land Title Office will accept. This should be confirmed with a BC lawyer or notary before assuming an out-of-province POA will work for a BC property sale.
How long does it typically take to gather everything?
It varies enormously depending on how organized the deceased's or incapacitated person's records were, and how quickly the court processes the application. Starting the document-gathering process early, even before a firm decision to sell, saves real time later.
