How Long Does Probate Take Before You Can Sell?

The honest answer, in plain language — plus how to not just wait around the whole time.

The short version: probate typically takes 3 to 6 months from when the application is formally filed, sometimes longer for a complicated estate. There's also a required 21-day waiting period after notifying beneficiaries before that application can even be submitted. The good news: in many cases, you don't have to wait for the Grant to be fully issued before starting the real estate process.

Where the time actually goes

Before an executor can file for probate, BC law requires giving written notice to all beneficiaries and the deceased's next of kin, then waiting a minimum of 21 days. Once the application is formally filed, the Supreme Court registry typically takes 3 to 6 months to issue the Grant — though incomplete paperwork, a complex estate, or a busy registry can push this well past 6 months. See our full probate explained guide for the legal detail behind each step.

You don't necessarily have to just wait

One of the most common misconceptions is that nothing can happen with the house until probate is fully granted. In many situations, a home can be listed and shown while probate is still in progress, with the sale structured to complete once the Grant comes through — rather than losing months of marketing time by waiting for the paperwork first.

What actually extends the timeline

  • Incomplete or inaccurate paperwork in the initial application
  • Disputes among beneficiaries or a contested will
  • A complex estate with many assets or unclear ownership
  • A backlog at the specific court registry handling the file

Common Questions

Probate Timeline — FAQ

How long does probate take in BC?
Typically 3 to 6 months from when the application is formally filed, though complex estates or paperwork issues can push this past a year. There's also a required 21-day waiting period after notifying beneficiaries before the application can even be filed.
Can I sell the house while waiting for probate?
In many cases, yes — a home can be listed and shown while probate is in progress, with the sale structured to complete once the Grant is issued. Whether that's right for a specific situation depends on the details.
What makes probate take longer than expected?
Incomplete paperwork, disputes among beneficiaries, a complex estate with many assets, or a busy court registry can all extend the timeline well past the typical range.
Does the house sale timeline have to match the probate timeline exactly?
Not necessarily — with the right conditional structuring, marketing and showings can often start before the Grant is finalized, which is one of the more useful things a probate-experienced realtor can help plan around.
