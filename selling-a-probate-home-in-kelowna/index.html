Selling a Probate Home in Kelowna

What actually has to happen before a probate home can be sold, and what's genuinely different about the process.

The short version: a probate home is a property that belonged to someone who's passed away, being sold by their executor or administrator rather than the homeowner themselves. In most cases it can't legally be sold (the sale can't complete) until the court grants probate or administration — but it can often be listed, and even conditionally sold, before that happens. The process is manageable; it just has a few extra steps a normal resale doesn't.

What makes a home a "probate home" sale

If the person who owned the home has passed away and the property was in their name alone (not held jointly with a surviving spouse), it generally needs to go through probate before it can be legally sold or transferred — regardless of what the will says should happen to it. The person selling it is the executor named in the will, or a court-appointed administrator if there's no will. See our guide on dying without a will in BC if that applies to your situation.

Can you list before probate is granted?

Often, yes. A probate home can typically be listed for sale, and even go conditionally sold, before the Grant of Probate comes through — the key requirement is usually that the sale doesn't complete (funds and title don't actually transfer) until the court has granted the executor or administrator legal authority to sell. Our guide to selling before probate is granted walks through how that's typically structured, and our realistic probate sale timeline covers what the whole process looks like start to finish.

What's actually different from a normal resale

The core real estate steps are the same — pricing, preparing, marketing, negotiating. What's different is who's doing it and what they know. An executor is often selling a home they didn't live in, sometimes one they haven't been inside in years, acting under a legal responsibility to get a fair result for the estate's beneficiaries. That changes a few things in practice:

  • Disclosure — an executor usually can't speak to a property's history the way an owner-occupant could, which needs to be handled carefully.
  • Getting the home ready — probate homes often need more preparation before listing, sometimes after decades in the same hands. See our guide on preparing an estate home for sale.
  • Valuing it fairly — pricing a home the seller never lived in takes a different kind of market read. See our guide on valuing an estate home.
  • Timeline pressure from the estate side — beneficiaries, taxes, and the court process all run on their own clocks, separate from the real estate market's.

Common Questions

Selling a Probate Home — FAQ

Can you sell a probate home before probate is granted?
Often yes, with the right approach — a probate home can usually be listed and even sold conditionally before the Grant of Probate comes through, as long as the sale doesn't complete (the money and title don't actually change hands) until probate is granted. See our full timeline guide for how that's typically structured.
What makes selling a probate home different from a normal resale?
The seller isn't the homeowner — it's the executor or administrator, acting under legal authority from the court, often selling a home they didn't live in and may not know the history of. That changes disclosure obligations, timeline expectations, and sometimes financing options for the buyer. It also often means the home needs preparing and valuing with input from someone who's done this before.
Who can legally sell a probate home in Kelowna?
The executor named in the will, or the court-appointed administrator if there's no will, once they have the legal authority (Grant of Probate or Administration) to act on the estate's behalf. Until that authority is granted, nobody can legally complete the sale.
Does a probate home sell for less than a normal home?
Not inherently. A probate home sells on its own merits like any other property — condition, location, and preparation matter more than the fact that it's an estate sale. Working with someone who understands the probate side helps make sure the home is presented and priced the same way a normal sale would be, rather than discounted out of urgency.
