Selling an Inherited House You Don't Want

No guilt required. Here's a plain path through probate, clear-out, and sale, start to finish.

Reviewed by Mark Jontz, REALTOR® — 500+ probate & estate transactions, Royal LePage Kelowna Chairman's Hall of Fame inductee.

The short version: it's completely normal to inherit a house you have no attachment to, no time for, or no interest in keeping — whether it's out of province, in rough shape, or just not something you want to manage. You don't have to fix it up, clean it out personally, or feel bad about wanting it handled quickly. There's a legitimate, straightforward path to get there.

You still need the legal side sorted first

Wanting the house gone fast doesn't skip the legal process — probate (or administration) generally still needs to be granted before the property can be legally sold. See our guide on what happens after a parent passes away if you haven't sorted out where that stands yet.

You don't have to clean it out yourself

Clearing out a lifetime of someone else's belongings is one of the heaviest parts of this process, and it's genuinely okay to not want to do it personally. There are practical options beyond doing it all yourself — see our clearing-out guide for a realistic approach that doesn't assume you have unlimited time or emotional bandwidth.

Selling as-is is a real, normal option

You don't need to repair, renovate, or stage the home to sell it. Many estate homes sell as-is, and that's a legitimate strategy, not a lesser one. See preparing an estate home for sale for what actually needs to happen versus what's optional.

Living out of province doesn't stop any of this

If you're handling this from outside BC, the process doesn't change in a way that requires you to be here constantly. See our guide for executors living outside BC for what's genuinely different about doing this remotely.

If the goal is simply "make this done," say so. Mark Jontz works with plenty of executors and beneficiaries who want the most direct, lowest-effort path to a closed sale, not the most elaborate one. That's a completely reasonable thing to ask for.

This page provides general information only and is not legal advice. Every estate is different — please consult a lawyer or notary about your specific situation.

Common Questions

Selling a House You Don't Want — FAQ

I don't want the house or have time to deal with it — what are my options?
You still generally need the probate process to complete before the house can be legally sold, but beyond that, selling as-is without doing repairs or extensive clean-out yourself is a legitimate path — the house doesn't have to be made perfect first.
Do I have to clear out the house myself?
No — there are services and approaches that can handle this for you, and not every buyer requires a fully emptied home to make an offer, depending on the situation. See our clearing-out guide for practical options.
I live out of province — can I handle this remotely?
Yes, this is common. Most of the process, including probate paperwork and the real estate sale itself, can be managed remotely with the right people helping locally.
Can the house just be sold as-is?
In most cases, yes. An as-is sale is a normal, legitimate option for an estate home, and avoids the time and cost of repairs or staging when that's not what you want to take on.
