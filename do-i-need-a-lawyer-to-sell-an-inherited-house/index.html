Do I Need a Lawyer to Sell an Inherited House?

Short answer: probably, for the probate side. Here's plainly what that actually means, and what it doesn't.

Reviewed by Mark Jontz, REALTOR® — 500+ probate & estate transactions, Royal LePage Kelowna Chairman's Hall of Fame inductee.

The short version: if the house was in the deceased's name alone, you'll generally need an estate lawyer or notary to handle the probate application before you have the legal authority to sell. The real estate sale itself — listing, marketing, negotiating, closing — works much like any other sale, with a realtor and a closing lawyer or notary involved, same as a typical purchase.

Two different legal roles, often confused

An estate lawyer or notary generally handles the probate application itself — confirming the will is valid (or that you qualify to administer the estate if there isn't one), and getting the court's Grant that gives you legal authority to act. That's a different job from the lawyer or notary who handles the actual property transfer at closing, which is standard for any real estate sale in BC, estate or not.

What a realtor handles instead

Pricing the home, marketing it, arranging showings, and negotiating the sale are a realtor's job, not a lawyer's. For an estate sale specifically, working with someone who's handled this situation before matters, since the timing has to work around the probate process rather than a normal closing calendar.

Do you absolutely need a lawyer?

For the probate application, almost always, if the property was solely in the deceased's name. For the closing itself, yes, as with any BC real estate transaction. Whether you also want a lawyer involved beyond that is more of a personal comfort question — many executors find it worthwhile given the added legal layer on top of a normal sale, but the specifics depend on your estate.

Common Questions

Do I Need a Lawyer? — FAQ

Do I need a lawyer to sell an inherited house in BC?
Generally, yes, on the legal/probate side — an estate lawyer or notary typically handles the Grant of Probate application and helps confirm legal authority to sell. The real estate transaction itself works much like a normal sale, with a realtor and a closing lawyer or notary, not necessarily the same one who handled probate.
What does the lawyer actually do versus the realtor?
The estate lawyer or notary generally handles the court application for probate and confirms who has legal authority to act. The realtor handles pricing, marketing, showings, and negotiating the sale itself. A closing lawyer or notary then handles the actual property transfer at completion, same as any real estate sale.
Can I sell the house myself without a lawyer at all?
You'll still need a lawyer or notary for the property transfer itself, as with any real estate sale in BC, and almost certainly for the probate application if the property was solely in the deceased's name. Whether you need a realtor is a separate decision, but most executors find the guidance worthwhile given the added legal layer.
What does this typically cost?
Costs vary by estate and by lawyer, and we're not in a position to quote legal fees. See our guide on how BC probate fees actually work for the court-fee side, and talk to an estate lawyer directly for their own fee structure.
