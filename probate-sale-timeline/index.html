Realistic Probate Sale Timeline

How long the full process actually takes, start to finish — and why it's almost always longer than families initially expect.

The honest version: a full probate process in BC typically takes 6 to 12 months from start to finish, sometimes longer for complex estates. The home sale itself doesn't have to wait for every single step — but understanding the full timeline helps set realistic expectations from day one.

Stage 1: Initial application and notice period (first few weeks)

After gathering the will, death certificate, and required court forms, the executor's lawyer files a Notice of Proposed Application, which triggers a mandatory 21-day waiting period before the full probate application can be submitted — this gives interested parties a chance to respond.

Stage 2: Court review and the Grant of Probate (typically 6–8 weeks)

Once filed, the BC Supreme Court typically takes around six to eight weeks to review and issue the Grant of Probate in a straightforward case — longer during busy periods, or if the application has issues that need correcting.

Stage 3: Listing and selling the home

This is where real estate specifically comes in, and it can often begin running in parallel with the legal process rather than waiting for every step to finish. Mark Jontz frequently helps executors prepare a home for listing while probate is still being processed, so the property is ready to go the moment the Grant is in hand — see our guide on preparing an estate home for sale. In some cases, a conditional listing can even proceed before the Grant is formally issued, depending on the specific situation.

Stage 4: The 210-day consideration

After the Grant of Probate is issued, there's a commonly referenced 210-day period before an executor distributes estate assets to beneficiaries — this exists to allow time for potential claims against the estate. Importantly, this generally affects when sale proceeds get distributed, not necessarily when the home itself can be sold. This is a nuance worth confirming directly with the estate's lawyer, since it affects planning either way.

Stage 5: Full estate administration wrap-up

After probate is granted and the home is sold, finishing the complete administration of the estate — final accounting, remaining distributions, closing out the estate formally — commonly takes an additional six months or more, depending on complexity.

What actually extends these timelines

  • A complex or large estate with multiple properties or business interests
  • Difficulty locating or organizing the deceased's financial records
  • Disputes among beneficiaries, or a contested will
  • Involvement from the Public Guardian and Trustee of BC
  • Court backlogs, which vary by registry and time of year

Common Questions

Realistic Probate Sale Timeline — FAQ

Can I list the home before probate is fully granted?
In some cases, yes — a conditional listing can proceed with the sale completing once the Grant is issued. Whether this makes sense depends on your specific situation, and it's worth discussing directly rather than assuming either way.
Does the 210-day rule mean I can't sell the house for 210 days?
Not necessarily — this period most directly concerns distributing estate assets (including sale proceeds) to beneficiaries, not necessarily the sale of the property itself. Confirm this specific point with the estate's lawyer, since it has real planning implications.
What's the single biggest thing that delays a probate sale?
Incomplete or hard-to-locate financial records for the deceased, and disputes among beneficiaries — both are worth addressing as early as possible, ideally before the court application is even filed.
How much of this timeline can Mark Jontz actually influence?
The legal/court timeline is largely outside a realtor's control, but the real estate preparation and listing timeline can run in parallel rather than only starting after probate is complete — that's where real experience saves meaningful time.
